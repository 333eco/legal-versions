Draft of 27 November 2026.

These terms are between you and Nhia Kou LLC, which sells names on 333.eco. They cover holding an @name or a #tag on 333.eco, your free address, the page you write for it, the thanks people send there, and the passes sold there.

## Today, and when fees go live

**Today:** payments on 333.eco run in Stripe's test mode. No real card works and nothing is charged, so no further @name or brand tag can be held, and no pass can be paid for, until fees go live. Steward tags and shop tags have no fee and are held today.

These terms describe both what the site does today and what it will do once fees go live. Each section that involves a fee says which applies.

A name held before fees began has no paid year. Its holder may pay for its first year, counted from the day it is paid, by card or with a pass.

## What an @name is

An @name is a handle you choose: one name on 333.eco, held by one person. The yearly fee holds it for you and buys nothing else. Your own name, and how it is said, is free for everyone.

- It lives at `333.eco/@yourname`.
- You hold a registration. You don't own the name.
- One @name per person.
- Names are for adults.
- Holding a name needs a passkey on your account.
- Names use the letters a to z, digits, dots and hyphens. A dot and a hyphen count as the same, and so do letters that look alike, so no one can hold a look-alike of your name.
- Some names and tags are kept for the site itself and can't be held.

## What a #tag is

A #tag is the address of a company, a product, a place or a machine, at `333.eco/yourtag`. A person has a name; a thing has a tag. `@nike` and `#nike` can both exist, and a trademark claim can't take a person's @name.

There are four kinds:

- **A brand tag** is paid for each year (see "Fees"). A person reads each request before anything is charged. For the first 90 days after tags open to real payments, brand tags go to trademark holders first: a request needs a trademark registration number, and a person reads it. After those 90 days, the first valid request holds a tag.
- **A shop tag** comes free with opening a shop on HeartBank®, one per shop. Any further tag is paid for.
- **A steward tag** is free, for a river, a temple, a species or a machine with no company behind it, through a person who looks after it. There is no price on this path.
- **An agent tag**, for a company's AI, is not offered yet. When it opens, its page will always say it is an AI and name the company.

A tag is an address, nothing more. It buys no placement: nothing on 333.eco sorts by who paid. It says what something is, not that anyone approves of it. Each holder may hold ten tags across every kind, and more on request.

If two valid requests for the same tag arrive at once, a person decides, and the decision is logged. There is no auction.

## Fees

**Today:** the fee page shows these fees, and nothing is charged. **When fees go live:** they are charged.

The fee is per year, in US dollars, and is set by your card's country, using the World Bank's income groups:

| Your card's country | @name | Brand tag |
|---|---|---|
| Low income | $3 | $33 |
| Lower-middle income | $6 | $33 |
| Upper-middle income | $9 | $333 |
| High income, or a country not on the list | $12 | $333 |

- The fee is read from your card before you pay, and you see the exact fee before you confirm. Nothing else about you changes it: not where you are, and not who you are.
- The list is reread each July and published at `333.eco/rules`. If your card's country moves to a higher group, the fee for a name you already hold does not rise.
- A year paid with a card from a different country is priced by that card's country.
- The fee is the price of holding the name or tag. Paying it gives you no title, badge or perk: no perks and no membership.

## Paying

**Today:** the steps below run in Stripe's test mode, and only Stripe's test cards work. **When fees go live:** the same steps take real cards.

- You pay by card through Stripe, the card processor. Stripe sends your receipt to the email address you type in the payment form; 333.eco doesn't keep that address. Your card stays with Stripe.
- If the card you pay with is in a different fee group from the one you were shown, you are asked to see your fee again, and nothing is charged.
- A name or tag is held only once Stripe says the fee was paid.

## Your year, and renewing

**When fees go live**, this section applies to every paid name and tag. **Today** it applies only to years paid in test mode.

- A fee holds a name or tag for one year from the day it is paid.
- **Renewing is off unless you turn it on.** You turn it on by ticking "Renew each year" when you pay, or later in Settings (for an @name) or Your tags (for a tag). Turning it on later saves a card and charges nothing that day. Before you turn it on, the page shows the yearly fee for that card. While renewing is on, your card is kept with Stripe for the next year's fee.
- 30 days before any paid year ends, we send one notice, whether or not renewing is on. It goes by email, or, if we have no email for you, it is shown at your next log-in.
- If renewing is on, your saved card is charged once, for one year, at the fee your holding was priced at, between 3 days before the year ends and 2 days after. The next year starts where the last one ended. If the notice did not reach you at least 15 days before the charge, the card is not charged that year. If the charge doesn't go through, it is not tried again; Settings says so, and you can pay for the year yourself.
- **You can stop renewing at any time**, online, in Settings or Your tags. Your card is removed from Stripe at once. The year you have paid for stays yours to hold until it ends.
- You can pay for another year yourself whenever no more than a year is paid ahead. It is added to the end of the year you have paid, or counted from the day you pay if that year has ended.

## When a year ends

**When fees go live**, this applies to every paid name and tag. **Today** it applies only to years paid in test mode.

A name or tag ends if its year ends and no other year is paid. It is never confiscated for not paying.

After a paid year ends, the name or tag is kept for you for 90 days, and you can pay for another year in that time. During those days its page shows what an address nobody holds shows, and thanks to it are refused. After the 90 days the name or tag is released: its page, photo, recording, the passkeys shown under it and its ways to receive money are deleted, and any card saved for it is removed from Stripe.

You can download your page at any time.

A steward tag and a shop tag have no fee and no paid year.

## Refunds

**Today** nothing is charged, so nothing is refunded. **When fees go live:**

- If someone held the name or tag a moment before your payment finished, your payment is refunded in full, automatically.
- If a brand tag is freed after a dispute (see "Disputes and reports"), the unused part of its year is refunded.
- A pass whose payment is refunded or disputed no longer works.

## Passes

**Today** a pass can be paid for only with Stripe's test cards. **When fees go live**, it is paid for with a real card.

A pass is one year of an @name, paid for at the posted fee by whoever gives it.

- You pay the @name fee for your card's country and get a code to give to one person.
- The person who uses it pays nothing and needs no card. They choose their own name and hold it with their own passkey, or add a year to the name they already hold. The pass covers one year, whatever their country.
- A pass works once and has no end date.
- It says nothing about who paid: not to the person who uses it, and not on their page. The person who paid sees only that it was used.
- One person may have at most 10 passes paid for and not yet used.
- A pass works only while its payment stands. If that payment is refunded or disputed, the pass no longer works.

## Your page

- Your page is public as soon as you save it. Only you can write or change it. Search engines are asked not to list it unless you turn that on.
- A page shows no counts, followers, ranks or totals. There is no list or search of people, and nothing on 333.eco goes from a name to who someone is.
- Every @name page carries the same note under its heart: "This heart beats on every @name: each is held by someone who logs in with a passkey. A passkey shows they hold a key. It doesn't show who they are, or that they're only one person." A page opened at a free address carries the same note, saying "every page here".
- You can delete your @name, or your whole account, at any time. Deleting your account removes your page, releases your name and deletes the account; nothing can be restored.
- Don't use a page to impersonate anyone, to harass anyone, or for a slur.

## Thanks, and money between people

People can thank an @name, a free address or a brand tag on 333.eco. A thanks can also carry Kiitos, HeartBank®'s record of a thank-you, pressed with the giver's own passkey. Kiitos has no cash value: it can't be bought, sold, or exchanged for money. Nothing is owed for a thanks.

A holder may list ways to receive money, such as Zelle, KHQR, Wise or PayPal. A person sees them only after they have sent their words; no amount is suggested and nothing is filled in. Any money moves outside 333.eco, between the two people, on that service's own terms. 333.eco never moves it and never learns whether any was sent. A steward tag and a shop tag never show ways to receive money.

## Names and tags can't be sold or passed on

- An @name can never be sold, transferred or passed on. A pass gives a year of a name, never a name.
- A shop tag is never freed.
- A brand tag freed after a dispute may then be requested by the trademark holder.

## Disputes and reports

- **An @name** can be ended for impersonation, harassment, a slur, or getting around an earlier ending with another name. Anyone can report a page with "Report this page", or by writing to abuse@333.eco. A name ended this way never goes to whoever complained. A trademark claim can't take an @name.
- **A brand tag** can be disputed by whoever holds the registered trademark for it, or for bad faith. A person reads both sides. A tag freed this way refunds the unused part of its year, and the trademark holder may then claim it.
- **A steward tag** can be freed if nobody is looking after what it names, or it was asked for in bad faith.
- **A shop tag** is never freed.

When a tag is freed, its holder is told once, with the reader's note and any refund.
