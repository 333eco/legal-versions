# Privacy policy

Draft of 27 November 2026.

This policy is between you and Nhia Kou LLC, which sells names on 333.eco. It says what 333.eco keeps about you, why, who else handles it, and what you can change. The terms of service are at `333.eco/terms`.

## Your account

You log in with Log in with HeartBank®, at login.333.eco. That service holds your ways to log in. 333.eco keeps none of them: no email address and no phone number.

| What | Why | How long |
|---|---|---|
| An ID that Log in with HeartBank® makes for 333.eco alone | to know it is you when you come back | until you delete your account |
| When you last logged in, and whether you used a passkey | some changes need a recent passkey log-in | until you delete your account |
| A token from the gratitude ledger, made when you log in | so a thanks you send can carry Kiitos | until you delete your account |
| Your @name: the form you chose, and the folded form that keeps look-alikes apart | to hold it for you, one per account | until you give it up, delete your account, or it is released |
| Your free address, as Log in with HeartBank® gave it, a copy kept on 333.eco's server | to open your page at your address | until you replace it or delete your account (Log in with HeartBank® keeps the address itself) |
| Whether you said you are 18 or older, 13 to 17, or under 13, when, and which version of these terms was in force | to let you turn on a page at your free address | until you delete your account |
| Your switches for your address: whether it sends people on to your @name, and what happens to your page after your death | to do what you chose | until you change them or delete your account |

Your language is kept in your browser. Once you are logged in, your choice is also kept at login.333.eco, which uses it for the emails it sends you.

## Your page

Your page is public as soon as you save it. Anyone with its address can read it, and apps can read it through the Look up API, one exact name at a time. There is no list or search of people on 333.eco.

Once you turn it on, your page also opens at your free address. An address leads to a page only through 333.eco's own server, one exact address at a time, with a limit on how many a visitor may ask for; no record anyone can read joins an address to an @name or to you. Visit counting records an address page as "/B:*", never the address.

A page holds only what you write or add:

- your name as you want it said, a short line and up to three links;
- one photo of yourself, if you add one; its details (such as where it was taken) are removed in your browser before it is sent;
- your own recording of your name, if you add one;
- your B-Autograph℠, if you draw one, kept as the points of the drawing;
- your language, only if you choose to show it;
- whether search engines may list it.

The photo and the recording are kept at storage.333.eco as private files, held under your account's ID and never under your @name. 333.eco opens only the version your saved page shows. Cloudflare keeps a copy of a shown photo or recording for at most a day so pages load quickly, and that copy is cleared as soon as the page stops showing it.

When someone shares the address of your page, the link preview shows your photo, your name and your line.

**Search engines.** Your page asks Google, Bing, Yahoo and Yandex not to list it, unless you turn listing on. No person's page is in the site's map for search engines.

## Passkeys shown under your name

You may show which of your passkeys sign as your @name, so a message you sign can be checked against it. Each one you show adds a public, permanent record at log.333.eco: your @name, that passkey's public key and the date. Removing it from your page, or giving up the name, ends what the page shows; the public record stays, with the dates it was shown. The name you gave each device is kept privately, for you alone.

You may also show a passkey on the page at your free address. Its public record names neither you nor your address: it holds a fingerprint of one line naming your address with a random value added, so nobody can find addresses by trying them. That random value is shown only to someone who opens your exact address, on your page and in Look up. Show the same passkey under your @name too, and anyone who has both can see they are one person. Replacing your address takes the passkeys shown there off your page; their public records stay.

Each change to the passkeys shown, and to the ways you receive money, is first announced to you through login.333.eco: an email to each address you linked there, or a note at your next log-in. 333.eco tells login.333.eco only which kind of change it was, never your @name.

## Ways to receive money

You may list ways to receive money, such as Zelle, KHQR, Wise or PayPal, with what each needs (for example a phone number, a username or a KHQR code). They are never on your public page, in a link preview or in the Look up API. A person sees them only after they have sent you their words. Some services show the payer more than you typed: Zelle and KHQR show your legal name or phone number. Any money moves outside 333.eco, and 333.eco never learns whether any was sent.

## Fees, renewing and passes

**Today** payments run in Stripe's test mode and nothing is charged. The same records are kept for test payments.

| Each pass: its code, who paid for it, the payment, the amount and the fee group, and once used, by whom, for which name, and which version of the terms they accepted | so a pass works once, and only while its payment stands | set out in the final version |

Your card's country sets your fee. 333.eco reads that country from the card before you pay, and keeps only the fee group, never the card or its country.

**What Stripe keeps.** You pay through Stripe, the card processor. Your card stays with Stripe. Stripe also receives the email address you type in the payment form, the name or tag you are paying for, and the amount. Stripe emails your receipt to that address; 333.eco doesn't keep it. If you turn renewing on, Stripe keeps your card, in a customer record with that email address and the name or tag, for next year's fee. Stopping renewing removes the card from Stripe at once; the customer record stays at Stripe. Deleting your account deletes your customer record at Stripe, with any card saved there. Stripe handles all of this under its own privacy policy.

The person who uses a pass learns nothing about who paid for it, and neither does their page. The person who paid sees only that it was used.

## Thanks

When you send a thanks, your words go to two lists at once: your Thanks Sent and the other person's Thanks Received. Each copy holds the words, the time, and the other person's @name, free address or tag, never their account. Someone with no @name is shown by their free address only while their page is on; otherwise a thanks from them reads "From someone".

- A thanks sent without your name holds your account sealed, so the person you thanked never learns who you are; it can be opened only by 333.eco's server, to let them hide your thanks or thank you back.
- If someone hides thanks from a sender, 333.eco keeps a fingerprint of the pair, never the names.
- A thanks can carry Kiitos, pressed with your passkey at sign.333.eco. The gratitude ledger then receives the Kiitos with your token and the other person's. Your words never go to the ledger. Your copy keeps the ledger's receipt ID.
- While a thanks waits for your press, a short record of it is kept, and it is deleted automatically after it expires. A day's records of who thanked, kept as fingerprints that name no one, expire after two days and are then deleted automatically.
- A thanks sent to a child's page is also shown to the parent or guardian who looks after it: they get their own copy, with the words, the time and how it was signed, never the sealed sender of a thanks sent without a name.
- If you delete your account, the other person's copy of each thanks stays theirs.

**Your thanks on the Earth.** Only if you turn it on, or add a thanks yourself. Your phone turns its location into an area about 100 km across and drops the exact place there and then. That area goes to the gratitude ledger with a one-use token that names no one, and adds to that day's public total at earth.333.eco. We keep no area, and nothing on the Earth points back to you or to a thanks. An area with fewer than two is not shown. 333.eco keeps only your switch, and whether each thanks was added.

## Alerts

If you turn alerts on for a phone or computer, 333.eco keeps the address your browser's push service gave it, the keys to encrypt an alert to it, the language and the time it was turned on, for at most 10 devices. They are kept until you turn alerts off there, log out there, or the push service says the address is gone. The push service your browser uses (Google, Apple, Mozilla or Microsoft) carries each alert; an alert never carries the words of a thanks.

Every alert is also kept for the bell at the top of 333.eco, whether or not you turned alerts on anywhere: what kind of alert it is, who it names when it names someone (the name or address a thanks came from, or the person asking to look after a child's page), the time, and the page it opens. Never the words of a thanks. Only you can read them; Clear all deletes them, they are deleted after 90 days, and deleting your account deletes them. When you open the bell, 333.eco keeps the time, so the dot shows only what came after.

Anyone can report a page with "Report this page", or by writing to abuse@333.eco. A report arrives as an email. How long reports are kept is set out in the final version.

## Visit counting

Visits are counted without cookies. Three counters run on 333.eco:

- **333.eco's own record.** Each page opened, and a few things done (such as a page saved, a fee shown or a thanks sent), go into 333.eco's database: what happened, the page's address (a person's page only as `/@`, with no name), the fee group for a fee, a random ID kept in your browser, your browser's name and version, and your time zone. It carries no account ID; older records that carried one are deleted with your account. When something goes wrong in the page, the error's message goes too. How long these are kept is set out in the final version.
- **The visit counter at thonly.org.** Each page tells it which page was opened (a person's page only as `/@`), the address you came from (a person's page only as `/@`), the source tag of the link you arrived by, if it has one, and where a link led (never a link on a person's page, except its invitation to hold an @name, which is the same on every page, and never after a thanks). With it go a different random ID kept in your browser, your time zone, whether a robot drives the browser, and whether the visit is our own team's. That ID is never linked to an account.
- **Cloudflare Web Analytics.** Cloudflare's script is never loaded on a person's page (an @name), and a page opened from one is told only 333.eco's address, so it never sees whose page was opened. On the site's other pages it reports each visit, with the page's full address, under Cloudflare's own policy.

On the team's own browsers, a cookie set by login.333.eco marks the visit as the team's, so it is not counted as a visitor's. It holds no identity.

## Cookies and your browser

333.eco sets one cookie of its own: while you log in, a cookie holds that log-in for at most 10 minutes, and it is cleared when you return. Your browser also keeps, on your device: that you are logged in, your language, light or dark mode, the two random IDs above, and what a page needs to finish a step after a log-in. There are no advertising or tracking cookies, and no advertising on 333.eco.

## Who else handles it

- **Google Cloud (Firebase)** runs 333.eco's database and server, in Singapore, and its log-in accounts.
- **Cloudflare** serves the site worldwide, keeps shown photos and recordings for at most a day, and counts visits to pages that are not a person's.
- **storage.333.eco** keeps the photos and recordings, under its own privacy policy.
- **Stripe** takes payments, as above.
- **login.333.eco** (Log in with HeartBank®) holds your ways to log in and sends 333.eco's notices.
- **sign.333.eco** takes each passkey press.
- **The gratitude ledger** receives Kiitos, as above. **log.333.eco** keeps the public records of passkeys shown.
- **The push service** of your browser carries alerts. **The visit counter at thonly.org** counts visits.

Google Cloud and Cloudflare may log the requests they carry, under their own settings. How long those logs are kept is set out in the final version.

We never sell your data.

## How long

The tables above say how long each thing is kept where 333.eco sets it. When a paid year ends and no other year is paid, the name or tag is kept for 90 days. After those 90 days it is released: its page, photo, recording, the passkeys shown under it and its ways to receive money are deleted, and any card saved for it is removed from Stripe. A short record that it was released (the name or tag, the account that held it, the date its year ended and the date of release) is kept. Anything not given a time here is set out in the final version.

## Your choices

- You can change or remove anything on your page, its photo, its recording and your B-Autograph℠, at any time.
- You can turn search engines' listing on or off, and show or hide your language.
- You can download your page at any time, with its photo and recording.
- You can hide thanks from a sender, and stop hiding them.
- You can stop renewing at any time, which removes your card from Stripe.
- You can see in Settings which version of the terms you accepted, and open that exact version at legal.333.eco.
- You can delete your account in Settings (the button reads "Delete this @name" while you hold one). It deletes your page, its photo and recording, your ways to receive money, the passkeys shown under your name, your lists of thanks, your alerts, your customer record at Stripe with any card saved there (so nothing renews), 333.eco's visit records that carry your account's ID, and your account. It keeps the record of what was paid (fees shown and payments), passes, and tag requests and decisions. It does not end a #tag you hold: Settings lists them before you confirm. The other person's copy of each thanks stays theirs.

How to ask for a copy of everything else 333.eco keeps about you, or for its deletion, will be set out in the final version.

## Adults, and children's pages

Names are for adults. 333.eco asks no one's age; when you turn on a page at your free address it asks whether you are 18 or older, 13 to 17, or under 13, and keeps your answer. From 13 you run your own page. Until you say you are 18 or older, it never shows ways to receive money, it is never shown to search engines, and you can't hold an @name.

A page for someone under 13 is linked to a parent or guardian only when both press their own passkey, using a code that works once, for 30 minutes. 333.eco keeps who looks after which page until the guardian ends the link or deletes their account; the child's page then turns off. A child's page shows nothing about them until the guardian turns it on. The child may write it too; anything they change turns it off again until the guardian looks, and the guardian and the child are each told on their phone when the other changes it (the alert says that the page changed, never what it says).
